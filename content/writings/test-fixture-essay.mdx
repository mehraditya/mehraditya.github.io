> **Temporary test fixture.** This is not an essay. It is a block of neutral prose with the shape of one, so the long-form reading layout can be judged before real writing is dropped into `content/writings/`. Delete this file when the first real essay arrives.

The point of a reading column is that you forget it is there. That only happens when the measure is right, the line height is generous enough to carry the eye down without effort, and nothing on the page competes with the sentence you are in the middle of.

This paragraph exists to be read as a paragraph. It has no argument to make and no evidence to offer. It is here so that the space between paragraphs, the weight of the type and the width of the column can all be checked against a body of text that keeps going for a while.

## Orientation, not decoration

Headings in a long essay are for the reader who is skimming, the reader who is returning, and the reader who lost their place three paragraphs ago. They should be easy to find and hard to notice. They orient; they do not announce.

### A smaller step down

A third-level heading marks a turn inside a section rather than a new section. It should sit close to the text it belongs to and far from the heading above it.

Images belong in the flow of the essay rather than beside it. A plain Markdown image stays at the reading measure:

## Links inside prose

Links should read as part of the sentence rather than as a button. The underline is thin, the hover is a darkening, and nothing changes shape. The About page is one click away, as is the Research Notes index.

A quotation in an essay is a change of voice, so it gets a thin rule and a slightly lighter colour, and nothing else:

> The interface should disappear. The thinking should remain.

## Ending quietly

An essay should stop. There is no call to action at the bottom of a page like this, no share row, no related-posts grid pretending to be a continuation. The article ends, the tags sit underneath it in a small mono face, and the footer arrives.
